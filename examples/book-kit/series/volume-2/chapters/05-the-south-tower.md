# The South Tower

The south tower was smaller than its twin, one hundred and four steps,
and warmer by a degree the keeper attributed to stubbornness. Its
keeper had kept the light for eleven years and the code book for ten,
and it was her correction in the margin that turned the singing fog of
the previous summer from an anomaly into a signal.

The two keepers spent one week under the same roof, comparing
registers, arguing about the weather, and adding the four hundred and
first signal to the code.

## What the Registers Agreed On

The registers agreed on the storms, on the fog, and on the exact hour
the engine of the supply boat had failed. They disagreed on everything
else, and both keepers considered this the strongest possible
foundation for a continuing correspondence.
